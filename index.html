Prosecution timeline and surveillance chain
Arrival, movement, rooftop approach, shot, flight, recovery

The prosecution treats surveillance as a chain of corroborating observations. The central defense question is how strong each transition is when examined independently.

Before the shootingState evidence describes repeated vehicle and campus activity associated with Robinson.
Shortly after noonA dark-clothed person with a backpack is described moving toward the Losee Center exterior staircase.
~12:15Surveillance is described as capturing a person at the top of the exterior stairs.
After the shotThe State traces a figure leaving the roof and moving away from the area.

Prosecution proposition

Clothing, footwear, physical attributes, gait, location evidence and later evidence collectively identify the rooftop figure as Robinson.

Defense / investigative test

How continuous is the camera chain? Which frames allow facial identification? Were physical descriptors documented before investigators knew the suspect’s identity?

DNA: enormous likelihood ratios, real limits
Contributor-level evidence is not the same question as activity-level proof
State evidence

High statistical support

  • Towel mixture evidence was presented with an extremely large likelihood ratio under the State’s contributor proposition.
  • The rooftop screwdriver evidence likewise produced very large statistical support.
  • ATF testing on rifle areas produced additional high likelihood ratios involving Robinson.
Important limitation

What DNA does not automatically tell you

  • When biological material was deposited.
  • The exact activity that deposited it.
  • Whether prior handling or transfer is a viable explanation for a particular item.
Open context

Lance-related mixtures

  • Lance’s biological material appears in portions of the evidentiary ecosystem.
  • That fact requires context; it does not independently establish complicity.
A giant likelihood ratio is not the same statement as “there is a one-in-X chance the defendant is innocent.” The statistic compares specified DNA hypotheses.

6
Rifle, projectile fragment, cartridges and toolmarks
Class consistency versus individualized firearm attribution

What the public hearing evidence supports

A recovered Mauser-pattern rifle chambered in .30-06, associated ammunition and a projectile fragment described with class characteristics consistent with the rifle’s rifling.

What the public hearing evidence does not establish

The damaged fragment was not presented as an individualized, conclusive firearm-to-projectile identification.

Do not collapse “inconclusive” into “excluded.” An inability to individualize the fragment is not the same as proof the rifle could not have fired it.
Do not inflate “consistent with” into “identified.” Class consistency does not equal a unique firearm identification.

7
Capital aggravator and third-party risk
One shot, one target, a public event, and a contested legal theory

State

A high-powered rifle shot toward a crowded public event created a great risk of death to people other than the target.

Defense

A single aimed shot from an elevated position toward an elevated target, on a downward trajectory, does not automatically satisfy the charged aggravator or its knowledge element.

The judge found probable cause. Trial requires proof under the higher standard and permits renewed attack on geometry, actual risk, knowledge and the controlling Utah law.

8
“Ask for a lawyer and stay silent”
Ordinary constitutional advice or evidence of concealment in context?
Defense reading

Standing alone, telling someone to get counsel, remain silent and require warrants is lawful and ordinary advice.

State reading

The State argues the message must be read beside alleged admissions, evidence-concealment discussion and an instruction to delete the exchange.

The judge found probable cause without deciding which competing interpretation will ultimately prevail at trial.

Andrew Wilson, Andrew Kolvet, Frank Turek and prosecution-friendly commentary
The cumulative-evidence argument and its own evidentiary limits

The strongest prosecution-friendly public argument is cumulative corroboration: surveillance, alleged admissions, physical evidence, DNA, the rifle and post-event conduct intersect.

The investigative test: how independent are those streams? Evidence derived from the same witness or shaped by the same identification assumption may be less independent than a simple list suggests.
Source discipline: “I’m told prosecutors have him dead to rights” is a reported-source claim, not personal knowledge or a court finding.
Expertise discipline: personal eyewitness knowledge, firearms expertise and legal conclusions are different categories.

Defense / innocence-theory vulnerability ledger
Questions are not affirmative evidence of an alternative shooter

Communications

  • If authenticated, the alleged admissions are detailed and highly incriminating.
  • Multiple alleged admission streams require a mechanism if a fabrication theory is advanced.

Physical evidence

  • The public firearm evidence does not affirmatively exclude the recovered rifle.
  • DNA likelihood ratios are large even though activity-level limits remain.
  • No perfect video is required to prove identity circumstantially.

Alternative theory burden

  • Lance’s DNA does not itself establish complicity.
  • A “patsy” theory needs a documented mechanism.
  • An anomaly in the official story does not automatically prove the opposite story.

Lance Twiggs: questions that can be asked without accusing him
Location, access, devices, tool ownership, DNA context and immunity

Lance is a central witness and appears in portions of the forensic record. No verified public court record reviewed for this companion establishes that he was the shooter or an accomplice.

Location / timeline

  • Where was he during the critical window?
  • What phone, internet, vehicle, work, camera or witness records corroborate that location?
  • Did investigators document an alternative-suspect review?

Physical / digital context

  • When did he last handle the rifle, towel, screwdriver or Dremel?
  • Who owned and routinely used those tools?
  • What exactly did the Lance-linked Apple/iCloud warrant produce?
  • Do native records independently authenticate the messages?

Two-pass verification: claims that need narrower wording
The difference between a defensible statement and an overstatement
Overstated claimMore precise formulation
“The bullet was conclusively matched to Tyler’s rifle.”Public hearing evidence described class consistency; the damaged fragment was not presented as an individualized firearm identification.
“The bullet was proven not to come from that rifle.”An inconclusive/non-individualized comparison is not an exclusion.
“DNA proves Tyler fired the gun.”DNA strongly supports specified contributor propositions on probative items but does not by itself prove the exact activity or time of deposition.
“Lance’s DNA proves involvement.”It creates a contextual question; it does not itself prove participation in the homicide.
“The judge proved Tyler guilty.”The judge found probable cause for bindover and expressly did not adjudicate guilt.
“Police only had screenshots.”Later device/provider warrants existed; the public record still does not contain every return.
“A hole in the official account proves an alternative theory.”A contradiction can weaken one proposition; the alternative still requires affirmative evidence.
16
What a clean-slate investigation would demand
Evidence collection and validation checklist

Timeline and identity

  • One master synchronized clock.
  • Native surveillance with documented gaps.
  • Blind or minimally biased physical-description review where possible.
  • Complete vehicle and infotainment artifact reconstruction.

Digital authentication

  • Sender and recipient devices.
  • Cloud/provider records.
  • Application databases, session records and server timestamps.
  • Location and network artifacts.

Forensic chain

  • Rifle, towel, screwdriver, cartridges and Dremel chain of custody.
  • Full DNA hypotheses and limitations.
  • Full firearm/toolmark conclusions and limitations.
  • Autopsy and projectile-fragment documentation.

Alternative-suspect discipline

  • Investigate material leads.
  • Record exculpatory findings as clearly as inculpatory findings.
  • Do not convert “unknown” into a preferred narrative.
17
Where the case stands now
What each side has to prove next
Prosecution

From probable cause to proof

Authenticate the communications, withstand surveillance cross-examination, explain DNA without overstating it, accurately frame firearm limitations, account for Lance-related forensic context, and prove each charged aggravator under the trial standard.

Defense

From questions to reasonable doubt

Show why specific weaknesses materially undermine the State’s cumulative case. If advancing an alternative shooter or frame-up, provide affirmative evidence and a credible mechanism rather than relying on unanswered questions alone.

The core question is not how many bricks are listed. It is how reliable and independent each brick is—and what the structure looks like after cross-examination.
