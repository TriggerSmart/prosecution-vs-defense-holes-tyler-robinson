What has the defense actually conceded — and what does “not guilty” mean?
Alternative arguments, stipulations, Cellebrite authentication, and what a plea does — and does not — establish

Core rule: discussing the State's evidence is not the same as stipulating that it is true, authentic, admissible, or attributable to Tyler Robinson at trial. Defense lawyers can argue conditionally and in the alternative.

Not a concession

“Even if” arguments

A defense lawyer may argue: “Even if Mr. Robinson was the shooter, the State has not proved this aggravator,” or “Even if this DNA profile includes Tyler, it does not establish when the material was deposited.” Those arguments test a separate element without necessarily conceding the premise.

Actual concession

Stipulations are different

A stipulation is an agreement on the record about a fact or issue. The reviewed record does not show a blanket stipulation that Tyler was the shooter, that every text was authored by him, that every location conclusion is accurate, or that every DNA inference is accepted for trial.

Texts: The defense had standing objections to the text exhibits. On Day 4, when the State moved to admit the thread, counsel said there were no additional objections beyond those already preserved. That is not equivalent to conceding authenticity or every inference.
Cellebrite: Agent Brian Davis testified that Lance Twiggs's phone was taken into evidence, processed at the Regional Computer Forensic Laboratory, and downloaded using Cellebrite. Davis said he personally reviewed the extraction and saw the Tyler/Lance thread in it. This materially strengthens the record beyond the simplified claim that investigators only photographed a phone screen.
Still challengeable at trial: Completeness, sender/device attribution, account control, provider-side records, timestamps, deleted artifacts, chain of custody, foundation, admissibility, expert methodology, and the meaning the State asks the jury to draw can still be litigated.
Message-timeline question: Courtroom reporting noted that photographs appeared to show an earlier 4:33 p.m. exchange while Davis described the first relevant Cellebrite message as around 11:00 p.m. That discrepancy should be resolved from the extraction and underlying records rather than assumed to prove fabrication or dismissed without explanation.
DNA and location evidence: Arguing about what DNA or location data proves does not automatically concede the prosecution's full interpretation. The defense can accept a premise temporarily, challenge only its significance, or preserve a broader foundation/admissibility challenge for trial.

What a not-guilty plea means

A not-guilty plea is procedural: it puts the prosecution to its burden. It is not evidence of innocence, and it does not predict whether the case will end in acquittal, conviction, dismissal, or a later guilty plea.

Rifle, projectile fragment, cartridges and toolmarks
Class consistency versus individualized firearm attribution

Script-matched documents & sources

Defense ballistics motion / ATF materialState bindover memorandum — Dkt. 702Final hearing transcript

What the public hearing evidence supports

A recovered Mauser-pattern rifle chambered in .30-06, associated ammunition and a projectile fragment described with class characteristics consistent with the rifle’s rifling.

What the public hearing evidence does not establish

The damaged fragment was not presented as an individualized, conclusive firearm-to-projectile identification.

Do not collapse “inconclusive” into “excluded.” An inability to individualize the fragment is not the same as proof the rifle could not have fired it.
Do not inflate “consistent with” into “identified.” Class consistency does not equal a unique firearm identification.

The strongest prosecution-friendly public argument is cumulative corroboration: surveillance, alleged admissions, physical evidence, DNA, the rifle and post-event conduct intersect.

Questions are not affirmative evidence of an alternative shooter

Written bindover ruling — Dkt. 739State bindover memorandum — Dkt. 702Defense preliminary-hearing brief — Dkt. 706
Plea posture

Not guilty is not exculpatory evidence

High-profile defendants have followed every path after an initial not-guilty posture: later guilty plea, trial conviction, acquittal, dismissal or other resolution. The plea itself does not tell the jury what the evidence proves.

Communications

  • If authenticated, the alleged admissions are detailed and highly incriminating.
  • Multiple alleged admission streams require a mechanism if a fabrication theory is advanced.

Physical evidence

  • The public firearm evidence does not affirmatively exclude the recovered rifle.
  • DNA likelihood ratios are large even though activity-level limits remain.
  • No perfect video is required to prove identity circumstantially.

Alternative theory burden

  • Lance’s DNA does not itself establish complicity.
  • A “patsy” theory needs a documented mechanism.
  • An anomaly in the official story does not automatically prove the opposite story.

Two-pass verification: claims that need narrower wording
The difference between a defensible statement and an overstatement

Script-matched documents & sources

Written bindover ruling — Dkt. 739Final hearing transcriptDefense ballistics motion / ATF material
Overstated claimMore precise formulation
“The bullet was conclusively matched to Tyler’s rifle.”Public hearing evidence described class consistency; the damaged fragment was not presented as an individualized firearm identification.
“The bullet was proven not to come from that rifle.”An inconclusive/non-individualized comparison is not an exclusion.
“DNA proves Tyler fired the gun.”DNA strongly supports specified contributor propositions on probative items but does not by itself prove the exact activity or time of deposition.
“Lance’s DNA proves involvement.”It creates a contextual question; it does not itself prove participation in the homicide.
“The judge proved Tyler guilty.”The judge found probable cause for bindover and expressly did not adjudicate guilt.
“Police only had screenshots.”Later device/provider warrants existed; the public record still does not contain every return.
“A hole in the official account proves an alternative theory.”A contradiction can weaken one proposition; the alternative still requires affirmative evidence.

Timeline and identity

  • One master synchronized clock.
  • Native surveillance with documented gaps.

Digital authentication

  • Sender and recipient devices.

Forensic chain

  • Rifle, towel, screwdriver, cartridges and Dremel chain of custody.

Alternative-suspect discipline

  • Investigate material leads.
  • Record exculpatory findings as clearly as inculpatory findings.
  • Do not convert “unknown” into a preferred narrative.

Where the case stands now
What each side has to prove next
Trial posture: The defense's preliminary-hearing discussion of texts, DNA, location evidence, “the shooter,” or shot geometry should not be treated as a blanket concession. Unless a fact was actually stipulated, the defense may still contest authenticity, admissibility, attribution, methodology, chain of custody, completeness or evidentiary weight at trial.

Script-matched documents & sources

Written bindover ruling — Dkt. 739Final hearing transcriptLegal-document archive
Prosecution

From probable cause to proof

Authenticate the communications, withstand surveillance cross-examination, explain DNA without overstating it, accurately frame firearm limitations, account for Lance-related forensic context, and prove each charged aggravator under the trial standard.

Defense

From questions to reasonable doubt

Show why specific weaknesses materially undermine the State’s cumulative case. If advancing an alternative shooter or frame-up, provide affirmative evidence and a credible mechanism rather than relying on unanswered questions alone.

The core question is not how many bricks are listed. It is how reliable and independent each brick is—and what the structure looks like after cross-examination.
